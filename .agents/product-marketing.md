# Verbatim — Product Marketing Context

> The read-first file for ALL marketing copy (site, emails, outreach, decks).
> The `.claude/skills/` marketing skills read this before writing anything;
> so should every session that touches copy. Facts here are verified against
> the product — never write a claim that isn't in this file or the code.

## Positioning (rewritten 2026-09-06 from the 2026-09-02 decision)

> Supersedes the 2026-08-13 positioning below the line. Reasoning lives in the
> vault: Decisions/Log 2026-09-02 ("go all-in on the brand-silent
> conversation") and Projects/SaaS/Strategy §The brand-mention gap,
> §Comparison handling, §Jobs we do NOT claim.

- **The problem, stated as a deficit, never as a category.** Every brand's
  evidence comes from people who already bought, answering questions the
  brand chose to ask. Analytics = what, never why. Reviews and surveys =
  post-purchase, solicited. Listening tools = mentions of a name that is
  rarely said. The decisions that matter are about the people you never hear
  from: the ones comparing, hesitating, buying something else.
- **The measured fact that carries the argument:** consumers barely name
  brands. In our own corpus, **4 brand mentions in 18,440 comments**; 80–86%
  of the conversation is about the category, not any company. This is the
  strongest line we own because it is measured, not positioned.
- **Competitive alternative (the real one):** still nothing systematic. The
  champion scrolls their own comments, misses competitors' entirely, decides
  from gut plus last week's anecdote. Secondary: listening dashboards (built
  to find a name that is not said), agencies/research projects (episodic,
  solicited), synthetic-research AI (simulated opinions).
- **The unique asset (the one-sentence answer to "what do you do that nobody
  else does"):** comment-level, category-wide, unprompted conversation on
  video platforms, including under other brands' content, with what is said
  in the videos read alongside it and evidence attached to every claim. Three
  properties, each a gap on its own: **comment-level** (not post/mention
  level) · **category-wide** (not seeded from your name) · **video
  platforms** (no public listening API exists there).
- **What comes back:** a prioritised weekly brief, pushed. What people want,
  what changed since last week, what to do. Every insight traces to a
  verbatim quote (exact-match invariant in code).
- **Value:** know what buyers actually think, weekly, without doing research;
  defend decisions with real customer words.
- **Segment / champion:** consumer brands ~$5–100M. Founder/owner for
  SA-sized brands; head of marketing for international mid-market. Write for
  the person who makes the call, not the C-suite.
- **Category handling:** we do not file ourselves as social listening. But the
  buyer will run that comparison in their head regardless, so we change the
  axis, never refuse it. The axes: what triggers coverage (a keyword you
  register vs the category conversation) · which layer is read (posts and
  mentions vs the comment threads underneath) · whether the video is heard
  (caption and metadata vs what is said out loud) · what comes back (a
  dashboard to interrogate vs a brief, pushed).
- **Say the losses out loud.** Listening tools beat us on breadth of sources
  (news, X, forums, reviews), real-time speed and crisis alerting, archive
  depth, and brand-mention alerting. Volunteering this is what makes the wins
  read as fact. On the site: say it generically ("listening tools"), never
  name a competitor. A public "Verbatim vs X" page is NOT allowed yet: it
  files us next to them permanently.
- **The line:** *"Every listening tool is built to find your name. Most of
  the market never says it. We read the conversation that doesn't."* Short:
  **"They hear your name. We hear the market."** Drop "only" everywhere:
  exclusivity claims are the ones that get caught.
- **Descriptor:** the wordmark keeps "Consumer Intelligence" as its small
  descriptor (nav, footer, title tag). It never leads a headline: it names the
  output, and an incumbent owns the phrase.
- **Retired frames:** "vs focus groups" as the opener (2026-08-13; keep as an
  answer, never a lead) · "consumer intelligence" as the problem statement
  (2026-09-02) · "video-powered" (never).

### Jobs we do NOT claim (2026-09-02; off the sheet, not demoted)

Never list these, even as extras. Each invites a comparison we lose on the
incumbent's terms: **why sales/conversion moved** (attribution tools own the
data) · **testing an untested idea, price or concept** (needs active panels)
· **perception tracking as a statistical instrument** (rolling panels with
confidence intervals) · **evidence to win an internal argument** (the
best-served job in the market already). "We also happen to surface X" in a
conversation is fine; a bullet on the page is not.

### Claims that are banned outright

- **The 84% dark-social figure.** Dark social is DMs and private sharing. We
  cannot see it either. One question ("so how do you see it?") ends the
  meeting. The claimable, different finding is brand-silent PUBLIC
  conversation: comments under competitor and creator content that never
  name a brand. Never merge the two.
- **"We read every comment" / "full coverage, not sampled."** Comment depth
  is a cost setting from the development phase, not the system's limit. Say
  today: "we read the comment threads, not just the posts, and go deeper
  where the conversation is dense." Say as direction: "depth is set by what
  the question needs." Any prospect can open a 12,000-comment video and check.
- **"Only we..."** Use "we read the conversation that doesn't say your name";
  never "only we can see it."
- Any testimonial, client name or logo. Any synthetic-consumer language.

## The buyer's own words (VoC bank — assemble copy FROM these)

Mined from public reviews of competing tools + persona research (2026-04/06):
- "I know my audience talks about us on TikTok but I have no systematic way
  to know what they're saying"
- "We're flying blind on our fastest-growing channel"
- "The tool gives us data but not decisions"
- "Doesn't tell us WHY people feel that way"
- "We still spend 10–20% of our time cleaning data and refining reports"
- "Would kill for something that does continuous focus groups from social
  data instead of one-off $30K research projects"
Usage rule: these phrases seed OUR copy (Copyhackers assembly). Never present
them on-page as testimonials — they are not quotes about Verbatim.

## Proof points (all true; the only numbers allowed on marketing surfaces)

- **4 brand mentions in 18,440 comments.** One brand's market, measured in our
  own corpus (Össur; never name the client). 80–86% of the conversation is
  about the category, not any company. This is the headline number now.
- One weekly sweep of one brand's market: **500+ videos, 5,000+ comments**
  (Össur run 2: 521 / 5,059).
- The funnel from that run, usable verbatim: **521 videos → 5,059 comments →
  120 themes → 5 recommendations**. ("→" is data notation and allowed in
  funnel/metadata strips; it is not prose punctuation.)
- Runs weekly on a real client's market today; reports ship on schedule.
- Every displayed quote is verbatim: exact-match validated against the
  source comment. Translation is never silently substituted.
- Setup input = brand name + competitor names. That's the whole form.
- NOT claimable: comprehensiveness ("every comment"), the 84% dark-social
  figure, scene/vision analysis, any testimonial, any client logo.

## Capability map (what scope copy may claim, as of 2026-09-06)

Scope copy must cover THREE audiences, not two: your brand, your competitors,
and the wider industry/category conversation, and it must lead with the
category conversation where the brand is not named. It may claim:
- **Live (verified in code 2026-09-06):** comments across TikTok, Instagram
  and YouTube · **Reddit** (threads and comments; subreddits auto-discovered
  per category, live since 2026-08-16) · **what is said in the videos**
  (caption tracks where they exist, transcription otherwise; grounds the
  analysis and is quotable evidence; say "what's said to camera", never
  "every video transcribed") · **news context** around the market (live since
  2026-08-11; secondary, never a headline) · owned-account data (follower
  movement, own posts, your audience's replies) · weekly delta layer (what
  changed) · relevance filtering + evidence floors (a theme only counts with
  enough independent voices; off-topic, spam and lookalike brand names thrown
  out before analysis).
- **Also live (product surfaces the site may show, verified 2026-09-06):**
  consumer profiles (personas with wants, blockers, triggers, how they talk,
  each with its conversation count) · the theme map (themes sized by
  conversations, gaining/fading/emerging week to week) · the face-off (you vs
  a competitor vs the category: share of conversation, sentiment, unanswered
  questions, themes each side owns) · the analyst (question mode: answer +
  what the evidence says + my read + not in the evidence; never invents a
  quote; silence is an answer) · document mode (paste a plan or brief; every
  claim checked against the conversation; supported / contradicted / silent;
  marks laid over the text) · reports: five starters (weekly digest, monthly
  marketing review, leadership one-pager, sales objections & competitors,
  content what to make next), scheduled sends with a recipient list and PDF
  attached, login-free share links with expiry and revoke, the client's name
  on the cover ("written for" free text).
- **Units:** say "comments" for the raw count (18,440), "conversations" for
  everything analysed. Never "voices".
- **In development (label it exactly that, never present tense):** nothing
  platform-level right now. Previous entries (Reddit, news) shipped in August.
- Update this map when capabilities ship; stale claims are the cardinal sin.

## Site structure rule (Heinrich, 2026-09-06)

The home page is not a spec sheet. Its job per screen: stop them, make them
curious, move them on. The headline and first screen never explain the
product; they make someone want to find out. Specifics (what we read, how
the brief is made, the evidence rule, what we are not) live on a second page
the home page sends people to. One idea per beat, short, and the design
carries at least half of each beat.

## Offer (design partners — matches the funding model, keep honest)

- **Five design partners** this round. Three months, then they decide
  (14-day exit). Locked launch pricing. Direct line to the founder.
- The exchange is explicit: they use it for real and give feedback every two
  weeks. Silent partners don't work — say so.
- Verbatim is new. Rough edges exist; partners shape what gets built. Never
  oversell polish; honesty about maturity is the trust play.
- CTA architecture: primary = early access (form: name, work email, brand +
  optional interest — never more fields). Secondary = show the product's
  output (sample insight/report), never "book a demo".

## Voice

Founder-direct. Plain. Confident without hype. Like Heinrich explaining the
product across a table — "Casual. Colloquial. Full of pronouns."
- **This but never that:** direct but never pushy · confident but never
  hyped · honest about being new but never apologetic · knowledgeable but
  never jargony · warm but never chummy.
- Contractions on. Active voice. Short sentences mixed with long ones.
  Specifics over adjectives. If a competitor could sign the sentence, cut it.
- Every line passes three tests: can the reader picture it? could it be
  proven false? could no one else say it?

### Exemplars (in-voice)
- "If you only need to look once a week, it's doing its job."
- "Your brand. Your competitors. That's everything you need to know."
- "A few brands at a time — if it's not a fit yet, you'll be told so honestly."

### Counter-examples (labeled, never imitate)
- ~~"Unlock actionable insights with AI-powered social intelligence."~~
  (too corporate: landing-page words, no picture, anyone could say it)
- ~~"We're revolutionizing how brands understand their customers!"~~
  (hype + exclamation + unfalsifiable)
- ~~"Verbatim isn't just a tool — it's a paradigm shift."~~
  (negative parallelism + buzzword; also false: it is a tool)

### Banned
- Landing-page words: unlock, unleash, empower, supercharge, elevate,
  seamless, effortless, game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionize, harness,
  streamline, leverage (verb), actionable, delve, robust, innovative.
- **Em-dashes ("—"), fully banned on marketing surfaces** (Heinrich,
  2026-08-13: "a dead giveaway"). Replace with period > comma > colon >
  restructure. The "→" arrow survives only inside mono data/funnel strips.
- AI-tell structures: "It's not X, it's Y" (max ONE deliberate use per page),
  rule-of-three triplets on autopilot, "Whether you're a X or a Y", empty
  openers ("In today's fast-paced world"), summary endings, fake authority
  ("studies show" uncited), invented case studies or personas.
- Hedging: "can help", "may enable", "potentially". Say what it does.
- Exclamation points. Title Case Headings. Emoji as decoration.

## Review loop for copy

Draft → Seven-Sweeps-style edit (clarity first, voice second) → tell-scrub
against the banned list → read aloud (kitchen-table test) → the three tests
line by line → human (Heinrich) signs off before anything ships.
